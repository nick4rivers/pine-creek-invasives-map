Pine Creek Invasive Plants 2026 survey

Pine Creek is the primary spawning stream for Eagle Lake rainbow trout. Meadow restoration across the drainage is reconnecting channels to their floodplains to rebuild the hydrologic resilience these fish depend on.

This map shows invasive vegetation surveys conducted in 2026. The results will be used to develop invasive species plans that work in partnership with meadow and channel restoration throughout the Pine Creek drainage.

About the survey

This survey effort focused on previously unsurveyed reaches of Pine Creek and its tributaries above McCoy Flat (Little Harvey Valley, Squaw Valley, Antelope and Gordon Valley). Vegetation surveys were conducted between May 19 and June 30, 2026, with data captured according to standards set by the Lassen National Forest. The work supports meadows already restored (Bogard/McKenzie, Logan Springs, Confluence) and those in design (Champs Flat, McCoy Flat), and informs future Forest invasive plant treatment planning.

Reading the numbers

  • Gross acres is the area of each mapped polygon. Many large polygons hold the plant at only a few percent canopy cover, so gross acres overstate the ground the plant actually occupies.
  • Net acres multiplies polygon area by the percent of the polygon the surveyor recorded as infested.
  • Survey coverage buffers every GPS track by 25 m on each side as an approximate search swath. The true search width varied with terrain and vegetation.
